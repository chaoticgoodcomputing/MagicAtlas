## Family 1: (BeginningOfTurnTrigger, TriggeredAbilityParser.Parse)

**Failure signal:** parser bails at `TriggeredAbilityParser.Parse` for 853 cards in this cluster. The parser doesn't recognize `At the beginning of [your] [step]` as a trigger condition.

### Cards in this family
1. **Broodheart Engine** — `At the beginning of your upkeep, surveil 1.` (cleanliness=0)
2. **Bloodcurdler** — `At the beginning of your upkeep, mill a card.` (cleanliness=0)
3. **Egon, God of Death // Throne of Death** — `At the beginning of your upkeep, mill a card.` (cleanliness=0)
4. **Astarion, the Decadent** — `At the beginning of your end step, choose one —` (cleanliness=0) — modal triggered; may need bail
5. **Quiet Disrepair** — `At the beginning of your upkeep, choose one —` (cleanliness=0) — modal triggered; may need bail

### Relevant rules
- **Rule 603 (Triggered abilities)** — "Triggered abilities have a trigger condition and an effect. They are written as `[Trigger condition], [effect]` or `When/Whenever/At [trigger event], [effect]`."
- **Rule 503 (Upkeep Step)** — "Part of the turn. This step is the second step of the beginning phase."
- **Rule 513 (End Step)** — "Part of the turn. This step is the first step of the ending phase."
- **Rule 701.17 (Mill)** — "To mill N cards, a player puts that many cards from the top of their library into their graveyard."
- **Rule 701.25 (Surveil)** — "To manipulate some of the cards on top of your library, sending some of them to your graveyard and rearranging the rest."

### Expected generalization
One trigger-condition recognizer that maps `At the beginning of your <step>` → `TriggerCondition { Timing: At, Event: BeginningOf<Step> }`. Step names map to existing `TriggerEvent` values: `upkeep → BeginningOfUpkeep`, `end step → BeginningOfEndStep`, `combat → BeginningOfCombat`, `draw step → BeginningOfDrawStep`. Effect parsing delegates to the existing effect cascade. If the effect parses cleanly, the family lands.

### Anti-patterns
- Don't add `Other` as a fallback `TriggerEvent` — every step name in our corpus maps to an existing enum value.
- Don't introduce a new ability kind (`BeginningOfTurnAbility` or similar). This is just a `TriggeredAbility` with an `At`-timing trigger condition.
- "Your upkeep" vs "an opponent's upkeep" vs "each player's upkeep" — different controllers. The trigger-condition `Filter` may need a `Controller` predicate. Inspect the gold to see what the fixture demands.

### Glossary gaps
None — all terms (`mill`, `surveil`, `upkeep step`, `end step`) are in `glossary.json`.

## Family 2: (AsLongAsCondition, StaticAbilityParser.Parse)

**Failure signal:** parser bails at `StaticAbilityParser.Parse` for 758 cards in this cluster. The parser doesn't recognize the `... as long as [condition]` suffix as a duration on a static-grant effect.

### Cards in this family
1. **Iymrith, Desert Doom** — `Iymrith has ward {4} as long as it's untapped.` (cleanliness=0)
2. **Sokrates, Athenian Teacher** — `Sokrates has hexproof as long as it's untapped.` (cleanliness=0)
3. **Merry, Esquire of Rohan** — `Merry has first strike as long as it's equipped.` (cleanliness=0)
4. **Giant Tortoise** — `This creature gets +0/+3 as long as it's untapped.` (cleanliness=0)
5. **Mistform Wall** — `This creature has defender as long as it's a Wall.` (cleanliness=0)

### Relevant rules
- **Rule 611 (Continuous Effects)** — "A continuous effect can have an explicit duration of 'as long as ~,' which means the effect operates only while the condition holds."
- **`AsLongAsDuration`** (existing) — `[OracleDuration("asLongAs")]` with `Condition: string` free-text field. The condition body is stored verbatim from the oracle text.

### Expected generalization
One static-grant recognizer that detects `... as long as <condition>.` as a trailing clause on a grant effect (keyword grant or P/T grant). Wraps the existing effect with `Duration: AsLongAsDuration { Condition: "it's untapped" }`. The Condition field is descriptive free-text — don't try to parse `it's untapped` into a structured predicate.

### Anti-patterns
- Don't invent a new `Condition` polymorphic type. `AsLongAsDuration.Condition: string` is the existing convention.
- Don't conflate `as long as` (Duration) with `if` (intervening-if clause, Rule 603.4) — they're different mechanics.
- Don't try to share parser code with `until end of turn` style durations; the syntactic position is different (suffix on the whole sentence vs. embedded after an effect).

### Glossary gaps
None.

## Family 3: (DestroyTargetSpell, SpellAbilityParser.SpellTapTargetRule)

**Failure signal:** parser bails at the lowest-priority spell rule (telemetry artifact — dispatcher records "last rule iterated"). The actual gap: no rule for `Destroy all <filter>.` mass-destroy spells.

### Cards in this family
1. **Armageddon** — `Destroy all lands.` (cleanliness=0)
2. **Supreme Verdict** — `Destroy all creatures.` (cleanliness=0)
3. **Creeping Corrosion** — `Destroy all artifacts.` (cleanliness=0)
4. **Flashfires** — `Destroy all Plains.` (cleanliness=0)
5. **Boil** — `Destroy all Islands.` (cleanliness=0)
6. **Serene Heart** — `Destroy all Auras.` (cleanliness=0)

### Relevant rules
- **Rule 701.8 (Destroy)** — "To move a permanent from the battlefield to its owner's graveyard."
- **`DestroyEffect`** (existing) — `[OracleEffect("destroy")]` with `Target: ObjectReference` field.
- **`ObjectFilter`** (existing) — supports type predicates (`Lands`, `Creatures`, `Artifacts`, etc.) and subtype predicates (`Plains`, `Islands`, `Auras`).

### Expected generalization
One new `[SpellRule(Priority = 60)]` named `DestroyAllRule` (or similar) under `Parsing/Parsers/Spell/Rules/`. Matches `^destroy all <filter>\.?$` and emits a `SpellAbility` with one `DestroyEffect` whose `Target` is an `ObjectReference` with the `All` quantity and a filtered scope.

### Anti-patterns
- Don't conflate with the existing `Destroy target ...` rule. `all` is a different `ObjectReferenceKind` than `target`.
- Don't extend an existing rule by adding `all` as a branch — adjacent-method merge conflicts are the exact thing the rule-split was meant to prevent. **One rule per file.**
- For subtype filters (`Auras`, `Plains`, `Islands`), check if `ObjectFilter` already exposes a subtype slot. It does — reuse it.

### Glossary gaps

## Family 4: (CreateTokenSpell, SpellAbilityParser.SpellTapTargetRule)

**Failure signal:** parser bails at the lowest-priority spell rule. The actual gap: no rule for `Create a [N/M] [color] [type] creature token.` token-creation spells.

### Cards in this family
1. **Pact of the Titan** — `Create a 4/4 red Giant creature token.` (cleanliness=0)
2. **Predator's Howl** — `Create a 2/2 green Wolf creature token.` (cleanliness=0)
3. **Roar of the Wurm** — `Create a 6/6 green Wurm creature token.` (cleanliness=0)
4. **Baloth Cage Trap** — `Create a 4/4 green Beast creature token.` (cleanliness=0)
5. **Hard Evidence** — `Create a 0/3 blue Crab creature token.` (cleanliness=0)
6. **Goblin Offensive** — `Create X 1/1 red Goblin creature tokens.` (cleanliness=0) — variable count

### Relevant rules
- **Rule 111 (Tokens)** — "A marker used to represent any permanent that isn't represented by a card."
- **`CreateTokenEffect`** (existing) — `[OracleEffect("createToken")]`. Inspect the record for its fields (count, P/T, colors, types, subtypes).

### Expected generalization
One new `[SpellRule(Priority = 60)]` named `CreateTokenRule` under `Parsing/Parsers/Spell/Rules/`. Matches `^create (a|<num>|X) <P>/<T> <color> <subtype> creature tokens?\.?$` and emits a `SpellAbility` with one `CreateTokenEffect` carrying the parsed attributes. Count: `a` → 1 (LiteralQuantity), `X` → VariableQuantity, numeric → LiteralQuantity.

### Anti-patterns
- Don't hand-roll a token-attribute mini-parser inline — if `CreateTokenEffect` already has structured slots (`TokenAttributes`, `TokenSubtypes`, etc.), populate those directly.
- Don't drop the color or subtype if absent in some lines — re-check the candidates; the simpler shape `Create a Treasure token.` would need a different rule.
- Variable counts (`X`): check the existing convention for `VariableQuantity` in `Quantity.cs` — if the spell carries an `X` in its mana cost, the count and the cost share the same X. Don't model that linkage in the spell rule; it's a card-level concern.

### Glossary gaps

## Family 5: (ReturnTargetSpell, SpellAbilityParser.SpellTapTargetRule)

**Failure signal:** parser bails at the lowest-priority spell rule. The actual gap: no rule for `Return [target|all] <filter> to [its|their] owner's hand.` bounce spells.

### Cards in this family
1. **Failure // Comply** — `Return target spell to its owner's hand.` (cleanliness=0)
2. **Reprieve** — `Return target spell to its owner's hand.` (cleanliness=0)
3. **Sunder** — `Return all lands to their owners' hands.` (cleanliness=0)
4. **Rebuild** — `Return all artifacts to their owners' hands.` (cleanliness=0)
5. **Saving Grasp** — `Return target creature you own to your hand.` (cleanliness=0)
6. **Evacuation** — `Return all creatures to their owners' hands.` (cleanliness=0)
7. **Void Snare** — `Return target nonland permanent to its owner's hand.` (cleanliness=0)

### Relevant rules
- **Rule 701 (general)** — "Return ~ to its owner's hand" is the canonical bounce phrasing.
- **`ReturnToHandEffect`** (existing) — `[OracleEffect("returnToHand")]` with `Target: ObjectReference` field. Existing rule(s) under `Spell/Rules/` may already handle the simple `Return target X to its owner's hand` case — confirm before writing a new rule.

### Expected generalization
One new (or extended) `[SpellRule]` for the cases not yet covered: `Return all <filter> to their owners' hands`. The `target` singular case may already work; check existing rules first. If both shapes need coverage and the existing rule is single-target only, add `ReturnAllToHandRule` as a sibling — one rule per file.

### Anti-patterns
- Don't modify the existing `Return target X to its owner's hand` rule if it's already passing for that shape — add a sibling for `Return all X`.
- `target spell` (Failure/Comply, Reprieve) is a different `ObjectReferenceKind` than `target permanent` — the filter should narrow to spells-on-the-stack.
- `your hand` (Saving Grasp) vs `its owner's hand` (Void Snare) vs `their owners' hands` (Evacuation) — different `Hand` destinations. The existing `ReturnToHandEffect` should have a slot for this; check before inventing one.

### Glossary gaps
None.
